Step 9 · Lesson 1 · Free

Go thy way and be reconciled — the direct amends and how to make them

The Covenant Context

When Jesus restored Peter after the resurrection — three questions matching the three denials, the specific and unhurried addressing of each one — He did not summarize or abbreviate the reconciliation. He went to each one. The restoration required the specificity. The going is the step. Step 9 is the doing of what Step 8 prepared. The list is in hand. The heart is willing. The conversation is ready. Now go. Step 8 produced a list and a willingness. Step 9 is where willingness becomes action. Colossians 3:17 KJV : "And whatsoever ye do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus." The amends of Step 9 are among the most concrete things the student will do in this program. The person doing them does so in the name of the Lord Jesus — not as a formula, but as an identity. This is who I am now. This is whose name I carry. This is how that name acts in relationship. The amend is the evidence of the turn. Every person on the list represents a place where the old life reached into another life and left damage. The repentant person who has been genuinely changed does not leave that damage unaddressed. This is not penance — it is the natural direction of a life that has genuinely turned.

The Direct Amends

“Go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.” — Matthew 5:24 KJV The instruction is to go. Not to wait for conditions to improve. Not to wait until the other person is also ready for reconciliation. Not to wait until the outcome can be predicted. Go. The going is the step — not the reception of the amends, not the outcome of the conversation, not whether the person forgives. The direct amends conversation has three parts — prepared in Step 8 Lesson 3: 1. Name what happened — specifically, without minimizing 2. Take full responsibility for your part 3. Where possible, offer to make it right Deliver all three. Do not stop at the first one. Do not allow the other person's reaction — positive or negative — to interrupt the delivery of all three parts. The amends is complete when all three have been honestly offered. The prodigal rehearsed his speech the whole way home. The father did not wait for the speech. He saw his son while he was yet a great way off and ran to meet him. The amends is not the performance — it is the arrival.

When It Is Received Well

Some amends will be received with grace — the other person is glad you came, the conversation produces connection, the relief is immediate and mutual. Receive this with gratitude and without surprise. God goes before the amends that is genuinely offered. “When a man's ways please the LORD, he maketh even his enemies to be at peace with him.” — Proverbs 16:7 KJV The person who has worked the steps honestly — who brings genuine humility and genuine accountability to the amends conversation — often finds that the reception is better than the fear predicted. This is not because the other person was easier than expected. It is because the posture of genuine reconciliation produces what the posture of self-protection cannot.

When It Is Received Poorly

Some amends will not be received well. The other person may be angry. They may use it against you. They may reject it entirely. The amends is complete when it is honestly offered — regardless of how it is received. The outcome belongs to the other person. The offering belongs to you. Do not repeat the amends in the same conversation in response to a poor reception. Say it once, honestly. The living amends — the change of life that follows — is the longer conversation. The words are the beginning.

The Road Forward

The pattern across Scripture is that genuine encounter with God produces restitution — not because it is demanded, but because the person who has been genuinely changed cannot rest in the debt. Zacchaeus gave back fourfold before Jesus said a word about it. Go to the next name on the list. Do not wait between amends for the results of the previous one to settle. The list was prepared for the going, not for intermission. Move through it with the same consistency that built the list.

What To Do When You Finish The Conversation

When the amends conversation is complete — all three parts delivered, honestly — leave. Do not prolong the conversation looking for a better reception. Do not return to the subject in the same visit to add what was forgotten. If something was forgotten, address it in a separate conversation. The amends is a specific, bounded conversation that has a beginning and an end. The receipt of the amends — gratitude, anger, silence, rejection — belongs entirely to the other person. Your part ended when the three parts were honestly delivered. Thank them for receiving you, regardless of how they received the content. Then go to the next name on the list. The person who has made honest amends is not the person who made the original harm. That person was governed by the bondage. This person is governed by something else — and the amends is the clearest possible evidence of the difference. The relational ground cleared here does not need to be recultivated. Step 10 is the daily discipline that keeps what has been cleared from filling back in.

Teacher Notes

Step 9 Lesson 1 PUBLIC LESSON. Step 9 begins. The tone is forward and practical. The student who completed Step 8 honestly is ready for the first amends. Move them into the going without extended processing.

Covenant Context For The Teacher

John 21 — Jesus restoring Peter. Three questions, three answers, three specific restorations — matching the three denials. The specificity of the reconciliation is itself part of the healing. Help students see: the direct amends is specific for the same reason Jesus was specific with Peter. The summary does not accomplish what the specific address accomplishes.

Matthew 5:24 — The Instruction Is To Go

Teach this simply. The instruction is not to wait. It is not to prepare further. It is to go. The preparation is complete. The going is the step. Help students who are looking for reasons to delay identify what the delay is protecting — and then send them.

Proverbs 16:7 — Better Than Expected

Many students arrive at Step 9 with the worst-case reception rehearsed in their minds. Introduce Proverbs 16:7 as the counterweight: God can make even enemies be at peace. The reception of a genuinely offered amends is often better than the fear predicted. This does not mean it will always go well — but the pattern of the genuinely offered amends is consistently better than the fear.

Pastoral Pressure Points

1. "What if they get angry?" Respond: deliver all three parts and then stop. Do not defend. Do not counter. Do not repeat. The amends has been made. The anger belongs to them. Your part is complete. 2. "What if I cry?" Respond: that is fine. The amends does not require composure. It requires honesty. 3. "What if they apologize to me in return?" Respond: receive it simply. You are not there to take inventory of their Step 8 list. Thank them and complete your amends.

Discussion Anchors

1. From Matthew 5:24 — "The instruction is to go. What is the last thing between you and the first appointment on your list?" 2. From Proverbs 16:7 — "What is the worst-case reception you have rehearsed? What would the best-case look like?" 3. From the three-part structure — "Deliver the amends for the first name on your list out loud right now. All three parts."

The Road Forward

The first amends is complete or scheduled. Move to the next name. Lesson 2 addresses what the amends produces in the person making it.
