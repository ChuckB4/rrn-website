Step 1 · Lesson 1 · Free

We admitted we were powerless — and why that is not weakness but the beginning of wisdom

The Covenant Context

The covenant story does not begin at Sinai. It begins in a garden, with a question God already knew the answer to — Where are you? — asked not because He had lost track of anyone, but because the person hiding needed to say where they were out loud. The admission is the beginning. It has always been the beginning. Every return to God recorded in Scripture starts at the same place: an honest accounting of what is actually true. Step 1 is that accounting. It is not the lowest point a person can reach. It is the first honest point. The person who arrives at Step 1 and says what is actually true about their condition is further along the road than the person who has been managing the appearance of everything being fine for decades. “I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.” — John 15:5 KJV Jesus said this to His disciples on the night before the cross — in the Gospels, the night before the new covenant was established in His blood. He told them plainly: without Me, nothing. Not less. Not less effectively. Nothing. The branch that is not connected to the vine does not produce less fruit. It produces none, and eventually it withers. This is not a criticism of the branch. It is simply what is true about branches. The admission of Step 1 is not merely a recovery statement. It is the beginning of what Scripture calls repentance — the honest acknowledgment that the life lived under self-governance has produced what it has produced, and cannot undo what it has done. Every step that follows is built on this admission. Every person is living under a name — the name of something or someone that has governed their life. The addiction gave its own name to the person: failure, hopeless, beyond recovery, defined by what they have done. Step 1 begins the long work of finding out whose name actually belongs on a person — and whose name the life was built to carry.

What The Text Shows

The branch does not apologize for needing the vine. It is a branch. The design requires the connection. A branch that understood its own nature would not strain to produce fruit through its own effort — it would abide. It would stay connected. It would receive rather than generate. Step 1 is the admission that the person has been operating as a severed branch — straining to produce through effort what only the connection can provide. That the life without God has produced exactly what a life without God produces. And that the person is done pretending otherwise.

Two Parts Of Step 1

The prodigal son came to himself not at the height of his strength but in a far country, among swine, having spent everything. That is where the turn happened. That is where it has always happened. Most people do the first part and never get to the second. The first part is the admission of powerlessness: I cannot control this. The second part is the admission about life: the life I built around the management of this has become unmanageable. The first admission is frightening. The second is devastating. And both are necessary, because the person who admits only the first has acknowledged a problem. The person who admits both has acknowledged that the entire operating system needs to change — not just the behavior that was most visible. “And when he came to himself, he said, How many hired servants of my father's have bread enough and to spare, and I perish with hunger! I will arise and go to my father.” — Luke 15:17-18 KJV The prodigal son came to himself. He did not come to a strategy, or to a resolution, or to a plan for doing better. He came to himself — meaning he stopped pretending. He saw his actual situation clearly, without the management and the justification, and what he saw was the truth. That moment of clear sight — that is Step 1. He did not wait until he had a plan. He did not wait until he was in better shape. He arose while he was still in the far country and started back.

The First Step Is Not The Worst Step

There is a fear that admitting powerlessness is the bottom — that saying I cannot control this means accepting defeat. It is the opposite. The admission of powerlessness is the removal of the last obstacle between the person and the help they actually need. The person who is still insisting they have this under control cannot receive help, because they have not acknowledged they need it. The prodigal son, in the moment he came to himself, was in a worse external situation than he had ever been. He was in a far country, hungry, among swine, having spent everything. And that moment — that worst external moment — was the turning point. He arose and came to his father. And when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him and had compassion, and ran and fell on his neck. The robe came upon arrival. The ring came upon arrival. The feast began upon arrival — not before, not in anticipation of a performance, but at the moment of the return itself. The welcome was not contingent on the quality of the speech the son had prepared. It was the response of a father who had been watching the road. Admitting powerlessness does not make the situation worse. It makes the return possible.

The Road Forward

This reveals a pattern that runs through every covenant God has made: the admission of what is true is always the beginning of what becomes possible. God has never started with a person's strength. He has always started with their honesty. Step 1 names the problem clearly: powerlessness, and an unmanageable life. Step 2 introduces the only sufficient answer to what Step 1 has exposed — the God who is able. The person who has honestly completed Step 1 is standing in exactly the right place to receive what Step 2 offers. THE WIND AND THE SOUND “The wind bloweth where it listeth, and thou hearest the sound thereof, but canst not tell whence it cometh, and whither it goeth: so is every one that is born of the Spirit.” — John 3:8 KJV Jesus said this to Nicodemus — a religious leader, a teacher of Israel, a man who came by night because he knew something was missing in what he had. Jesus told him what the new birth sounds like: the wind that cannot be seen or predicted, but whose sound is heard. Every genuine birth of the Spirit produces evidence that can be heard. This is not an advanced theological point. It is the first description Jesus gives of what the new birth is like. He does not describe it as a decision, a prayer, or a change of mind. He describes it as something that produces a sound — something whose presence can be heard by those who are present when it occurs. The person entering this program is beginning a journey that the steps are designed to clear the way for. What they are being cleared for will make a sound when it arrives. That is what the Scripture says. Keep that in mind as the journey proceeds. The person who has honestly completed Step 1 is not defeated. They are positioned. For the first time, the door is open — because they have stopped pretending it is not there. What comes through that door is not their effort. It is God's response to their honesty. Admitting powerlessness is not the end of the journey. It is the beginning of a question the next step is written to answer.

Teacher Notes

Step 1 Lesson 1 PUBLIC LESSON. The first lesson of the entire curriculum. The room will contain people at very different places — some in acute crisis, some functioning but broken beneath the surface, some who came because someone else wanted them here. Meet every person with the grace of 1 John 4:19. We love Him because He first loved us. The program does not begin with demand. It begins with love already given.

Covenant Context For The Teacher

John 15:5 is a Gospel passage — spoken by Jesus to His disciples on the night before the cross, while the new covenant was still being established. It describes the design of the relationship: the branch needs the vine. This is not shame — it is structure. Help students understand that admitting they need God is not a weakness of character. It is the honest recognition of how they were made.

The Primary Passage — John 15:5

Show what the text shows before drawing conclusions. A branch is designed to receive from the vine. It does not generate its own fruit. Ask: what does a branch that is disconnected from the vine look like over time? Let the room answer. Then ask: what has the disconnected life produced? This is not accusation — it is observation. The text shows the design. The life shows what happens when the design is violated.

The Prodigal — Luke 15:17-18

He came to himself. Teach the meaning of this phrase carefully. It does not mean he got his act together. It means he stopped lying to himself about what was true. The moment of coming to himself was not the moment of improvement — it was the moment of honesty. And the Father was already watching before the son turned toward home.

Pastoral Pressure Points

1. "Admitting I'm powerless feels like giving up." Respond: the branch does not give up when it acknowledges it needs the vine. It returns to the design it was made for. Giving up would be staying in the far country. Coming to yourself is the opposite of giving up. 2. "I've had worse moments than this — this isn't rock bottom." Respond: Step 1 is not about finding rock bottom. It is about honesty. The prodigal did not wait until he starved to death. He came to himself in the far country and started back. You do not have to wait for a worse version of today. 3. "I can control it when I really try." Respond with John 15:5. Without Me, nothing. Not less. Nothing. Let the text do the work. Ask: how many times has really trying produced lasting change?

Discussion Anchors — Drawn From The Text

1. From John 15:5 — "What has the disconnected life produced? Name something specific — not what you were trying to produce, but what the life without that connection actually generated." 2. From Luke 15:17 — "Describe the moment you came to yourself — when the management stopped working and you saw your actual situation clearly. What did you see?" 3. From Luke 15:18 — "He said: I will arise and go. What is the difference between saying that and doing it? What made the difference for you?"

The Road Forward

Step 1 has named two things: powerlessness and the unmanageable life. The student who leaves this session with one honest answer to each of those is ready for Step 2. Step 2 is not about trying harder. It is about encountering the God who is actually able — which requires understanding what kind of God He is and what He has promised to the person who seeks Him.
