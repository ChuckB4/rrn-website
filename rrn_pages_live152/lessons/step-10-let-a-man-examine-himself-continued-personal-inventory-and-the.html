Step 10 · Lesson 1 · Free

Let a man examine himself — continued personal inventory and the promptly principle

The Covenant Context

When Nehemiah rebuilt the walls of Jerusalem, the workers carried their tools in one hand and their weapons in the other — building and watching simultaneously. The work of construction and the work of vigilance happened at the same time, not in sequence. Step 10 is the posture of Nehemiah's workers — the daily examination is not a return to Step 4 as though the earlier work failed. It is the ongoing vigilance of a person who has cleared the ground and intends to keep it clear. Step 9 cleared the ground of the past. Step 10 is the daily practice that keeps it clear. The daily inventory of Step 10 is the practice of a person who has understood what name they are living under. Every day, the question the inventory asks is the same: did I live today under the name that has been placed on me, or under the old names the flesh still reaches for? The answer shapes the next day's starting point. Daily inventory is daily repentance made practical. The person who takes a daily inventory and promptly admits when wrong is practicing what Paul the apostle described as dying daily — the ongoing posture of a life that continues to turn toward God rather than toward self.

Let A Man Examine Himself

“But let a man examine himself, and so let him eat of that bread, and drink of that cup.” — 1 Corinthians 11:28 KJV Paul wrote this to born-again believers at Corinth — instructing them in the self-examination that the participation in the Lord's table requires. The examination is not annual. It is not occasional. It is the posture that accompanies each participation — the daily return to honest self-assessment before bringing what is there before God. Step 10 applies this same discipline to the daily life: examine yourself today. Not the accumulated inventory of years — that was Step 4. Today's inventory. The resentment that formed this morning. The fear that drove the decision this afternoon. The harm that was caused in the conversation this evening. Examine it today, while it is today. Paul the apostle wrote about dying daily — not from a position of arrival, but from the ongoing practice of a person who knew that the flesh does not retire. The daily examination is not evidence of failure. It is the practice of the person who has understood.

The Promptly Principle

“Therefore to him that knoweth to do good, and doeth it not, to him it is sin.” — James 4:17 KJV The sin of omission — the known thing not done, the wrong not addressed, the amends not promptly made — is identified by James as sin in the same way the sin of commission is. The promptly principle of Step 10 is the application of James 4:17 to the daily life: when you find you are wrong today, say so today. Do not accumulate a new inventory. Address it at the inventory's smallest unit.

Why Promptly Matters

The resentment held overnight is harder to address in the morning. The wrong not addressed this week is harder to acknowledge next week. The pattern of delay is the same pattern Step 4 revealed — the management of what is seen, the protection of the position, the avoidance of the uncomfortable honest conversation. Promptly is not only a discipline for the relationship with the harmed person. It is the practice that prevents the daily inventory from becoming the accumulated inventory that Step 4 required. The person who addresses today's wrongs today has no week-long backlog to manage. The person who defers accumulates a Step 4 that keeps growing.

What The Daily Examination Includes

Today's resentment — anything from today's interactions that produced the familiar feeling of re-living a wound. Name it. Was it justified? What did I do with it? Today's fear — what drove a decision that the honest examination shows was fear-based. Name it. What would the faith-based decision have looked like? Today's harm — any person who, as a result of today's interactions, now has something against me. Name them. What is owed? Today's omission — what I knew to do today and did not do. Name it.

The Road Forward

This is the pattern Paul the apostle described as dying daily — not a single event, but a practice. The ongoing inventory is not the evidence of a person who has not arrived. It is the practice of a person who understands that the flesh does not retire, and has stopped pretending otherwise. Step 10 is not a step — it is a daily life. It does not complete. It continues into Step 11 and Step 12 as the permanent discipline of the person who has cleared the ground and intends to keep it clear. Lesson 2 gives the four areas of daily inventory in practical detail. Begin the daily examination tonight.

The Examination Includes This Question

“But ye are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit, if so be that the Spirit of God dwell in you. Now if any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his.” — Romans 8:9 KJV Paul wrote this to born-again believers in Rome — giving them the mark by which the honest examination can assess its own standing. Not church attendance. Not moral improvement. Not the length of the recovery. The Spirit of God dwelling within. Step 10 is the step of continued self-examination. The examination is honest and it does not exclude this question. The daily inventory of Step 10 keeps the ground clear — and the cleared ground is precisely what makes it possible to honestly ask: does the Spirit of God dwell in me? Not as a doctrine I affirm. As a present reality I have received. The curriculum has been building toward this question since the first lesson. The honest answer to it — whatever the honest answer is — is the most important entry in any inventory. The person who maintains a daily inventory is not performing accountability — they are living as someone for whom honesty has become the default. That is transformation at the level of character, not behavior. That is what the steps have been building. The examined life and the prayerful life are the two disciplines that sustain everything the prior steps built. Step 11 is where the examined life finds its source.

Teacher Notes

Step 10 Lesson 1 PUBLIC LESSON. The tone of this lesson is maintenance, not crisis. Step 10 is the daily discipline of the person who has completed the intensive work of Steps 1-9 and is now learning to live in what was built. Ground it in the Nehemiah image: building and watching simultaneously.

Covenant Context For The Teacher

Nehemiah 4:17 — the workers built with one hand and held a weapon with the other. The vigilance and the construction happened simultaneously. Step 10 is the same posture: living the new life and watching the daily inventory at the same time, not in sequence. 1 CORINTHIANS 11:28 — HOW TO TEACH Paul wrote this to born-again believers. The self-examination that precedes the Lord's table is not an annual audit. It is the daily posture of a person who brings honest self-assessment into every significant act of participation. Help students see: Step 10 is the application of this posture to every day, not only to formal religious observance.

The Promptly Principle

This is the most practically important instruction in Step 10. Teach it clearly. When wrong today, say so today. The delay is the same pattern as the avoidance — it accumulates what should have been addressed promptly. Ask students: how long did the last wrong sit before it was addressed? What grew in the delay?

Pastoral Pressure Points

1. "I feel like I'm going backward — doing inventory again." Respond with the Nehemiah image. This is not going back to Step 4. This is the vigilance that protects what Step 4 cleared. 2. "I don't have time for a daily inventory." Respond: the daily inventory takes as long as the honest examination of today requires. It is not a formal session — it is the ongoing posture of the person who has learned to examine before the accumulation requires a formal session. 3. "I was wrong today and I haven't addressed it yet." Respond: promptly is today. Address it before the end of the day. If the person is in the session — give them the time to make the call or send the message before they leave.

Discussion Anchors

1. From 1 Corinthians 11:28 — "What is the thing from today that the honest examination shows needs to be addressed promptly?" 2. From James 4:17 — "Name the omission from this week. The thing you knew to do and did not do." 3. From the four areas — "Which of the four areas — resentment, fear, harm, omission — shows up most frequently in your daily inventory? What does the pattern show?"
