Preface · Lesson 1 · Free

Why this program exists — and why you cannot fix yourself

The Covenant Context

From the very beginning, God has been reaching toward His people. Every covenant He has made — with Adam, with Noah, with Abraham, with Israel, with David — tells the same story. God initiates. He draws near. He provides the way. And again and again, the people He loves discover that they cannot sustain their own obedience without Him. This program exists inside that same story. You are not the first person to have arrived at the end of their own resources. You are not the first person to have discovered that the life they built without God has a ceiling — and that the ceiling is lower than they thought. The entire Scripture is full of people who arrived exactly where you are and found that the God who made them was still there, still reaching, still able to do what they could not do for themselves. “We love him, because he first loved us.” — 1 John 4:19 KJV That is the foundation of everything in this curriculum. Not your effort to reach God. His love reaching you. Not your decision to get better. His decision to provide the way. The twelve steps that follow are the road of response — the obedience of a person who has received love they did not earn and is now walking toward the God who offered it. HOW MAN DIAGNOSES — AND WHERE THE DIAGNOSIS FALLS SHORT Man has always tried to explain the condition. The explanations change with the century. In one generation it is a moral failure — the person is weak, lacks character, has made poor choices. In another generation it is a disease — the person has a condition, a brain chemistry, a genetic predisposition. In another it is a trauma response — the person is wounded, reacting, surviving. Every one of these frameworks contains something true. And every one of them stops short of the root. The moral failure framework is right that choices matter and wrong that better choices are within easy reach of a person who has been bound. The disease framework is right that the condition has physical dimensions and wrong that medication and management are the deepest available answer. The trauma framework is right that wounds drive behavior and wrong that healing the wound is sufficient to change what the wound produced. None of these frameworks have a category for what the Scripture names as the actual problem: the flesh. Not weakness. Not illness. Not damage. The flesh — the nature that governs the person who is living outside of God's governance — and the bondage that the flesh, operating without the Spirit, inevitably produces. The flesh produces what it produces. It has always done so. And no framework that begins below the level of the spirit can address what begins there. HOW GOD DIAGNOSES — AND WHAT THE ANSWER REQUIRES Before the sound of John 3:8 can be fully understood, one question must be addressed — because it will arise and it deserves an honest answer. Some people will say: I was born this way. The appetites, the patterns, the drives that led to the bondage — they say these were present from birth, written into who I am. Not a choice but a condition. Let this be granted for a moment. If it is true — if the flesh and its patterns are present from natural birth — then what Jesus said to Nicodemus applies with more force, not less. “Jesus answered and said unto him, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God.” — John 3:3 KJV If you were born one way — and that way produces what it has produced — then the answer is not management of what you were born with. The answer is a new birth. A different kind. Born again — from above — of water and of the Spirit. What the first birth produced, the second birth addresses at the root. The argument that you were born with these patterns is not an argument against this program. It is the clearest argument for it. What natural birth produced requires supernatural birth to address. “Marvel not that I said unto thee, Ye must be born again.” — John 3:7 KJV Jesus did not say you might benefit from being born again. He said you must. Not suggested. Required. And every person who comes honestly through this curriculum will arrive at that requirement — not as a demand imposed from outside, but as the conclusion the honest road produces. “The wind bloweth where it listeth, and thou hearest the sound thereof, but canst not tell whence it cometh, and whither it goeth: so is every one that is born of the Spirit.” — John 3:8 KJV God asks the question twice in the same chapter. He does not ask it to be harsh. He asks it because the question contains the answer. If the first birth produced the condition, the new birth addresses it. That is the diagnosis. And this program is the road of honest preparation for it. From the very beginning of the covenant story, God does not merely describe Himself. He names Himself. And what God does with His name across all of Scripture is worth paying attention to — because the pattern does not end with the Old Testament. “And they shall put my name upon the children of Israel; and I will bless them.” — Numbers 6:27 KJV God did not say: they shall put a title upon them. He said: my name. The blessing follows the name. Not the effort. Not the attendance. Not the performance. The name placed upon the person. This is a thread worth following all the way through.

Who This Program Is For

“Now the works of the flesh are manifest, which are these; Adultery, fornication, uncleanness, lasciviousness, Idolatry, witchcraft, hatred, variance, emulations, wrath, strife, seditions, heresies, Envyings, murders, drunkenness, revellings, and such like: of the which I tell you before, as I have also told you in time past, that they which do such things shall not inherit the kingdom of God.” — Galatians 5:19-21 KJV This curriculum is not just a drug and alcohol program. It is a program for the works of the flesh. That list is long and it is varied. Drunkenness is on it. So is wrath. So is strife, envy, self-deception, and the pride that occupies the throne that belongs to God. The person who has never touched a substance but has spent a lifetime in anger, bitterness, or hidden sin is in the same program as the person whose substance use brought them to a visible crisis. The flesh is the problem in every case. The works differ. The root is the same. This program is not only for those in visible collapse. It is equally for those who are functional, stable, and outwardly composed — whose sin is private, whose bondage is internal, whose performance of faithfulness has never been interrupted by a consequence others could see. This program does not begin at a person's best. It begins at the honest bottom — and Scripture shows consistently that the bottom is precisely where God has always chosen to appear. “Woe to them that are at ease in Zion.” — Amos 6:1 KJV The warning is not to the person in obvious wreckage. It is to the person who is comfortable — settled in the appearance of faithfulness, undisturbed by the slow accumulation of compromise no one else can see. The person at ease in Zion is in greater danger than the person in crisis, because the crisis at least creates conditions for honesty. “Examine yourselves, whether ye be in the faith; prove your own selves.” — 2 Corinthians 13:5 KJV Every participant — regardless of background, history, or what brought them here — undergoes the same honest self-examination. There is no hierarchy of sin in this program. Pride and self-righteousness are not lesser offenses than public addiction. Hidden compromise is not more acceptable than visible failure. The examination is the same. The love that meets us in the examination is the same. And the God who does the work is the same. This is not behavior management. It is not image correction. It is the pursuit of genuine inward transformation — beginning with honesty about what is actually there. UNDERSTANDING HOW SCRIPTURE SPEAKS — THE LANGUAGE OF METAPHOR Before entering the lessons that follow, there is something essential about how the Scripture communicates. The Bible is not a collection of flat, literal statements. It speaks in metaphor — in images drawn from the physical world that carry truths about the spiritual world that plain language cannot reach. Understanding this is not an academic exercise. It is a survival skill for the person working this curriculum. The Scripture uses physical images to carry spiritual weight because the deepest truths are too large for simple declarative sentences. When the Bible says trees, it often means people. When it says water, it often means life or the Word or the Spirit. When it says darkness, it often means the condition of a life without God's light. This was not accidental — it was deliberate. Jesus spoke almost exclusively in parables. The prophets spoke in images. The wisdom literature is saturated with them. Here are ten metaphors the Scripture uses that the recovery road will illuminate again and again:

Trees As People

“Blessed is the man that walketh not in the counsel of the ungodly... he shall be like a tree planted by the rivers of water, that bringeth forth his fruit in his season.” — Psalm 1:1-3 KJV Trees in Scripture often represent people — their character, their rootedness, their fruit. When Zacchaeus climbed a sycamore tree, he was above the crowd — elevated in position, disconnected from the level ground of honest encounter with Jesus. When Adam hid among the trees of the garden, the image holds a deeper layer: hiding among people who gave him cover for what he had done — pointing to others, saying "they do this or that, so what I did is reasonable." The trees become the justifications. The hiding is not physical — it is relational and psychological. "Everyone does this." "I am not as bad as them." The trees of other people's behavior become the cover for our own.

Water As Life, Word, And Spirit

“Jesus answered and said unto her, Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again: But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst.” — John 4:13-14 KJV Water in Scripture carries multiple layers simultaneously: physical life, the Word of God, and the Spirit. The woman at the well understood the conversation to be about the water she carried. Jesus was describing what He alone could give. The person who returns to the same sources — the substance, the relationship, the behavior — returns thirsty again. The water that does not run dry is not drawn from the same well as what they have been drinking.

Fire As God'S Presence And Purification

“For our God is a consuming fire.” — Hebrews 12:29 KJV Fire in Scripture is the presence of God — burning without consuming what it does not condemn (the bush), or consuming entirely what is placed before it (the sacrifice). Refinement requires fire. The dross rises to the surface under heat. The things that cannot survive the fire are not the real thing — and the fire reveals this. The person in recovery is in the fire. What is rising to the surface is the material that needed to come up.

Salt As Covenant And Preservation

“Ye are the salt of the earth.” — Matthew 5:13 KJV Salt in the ancient world preserved what would otherwise decay. A covenant "salted" was a covenant that would not corrupt over time. When Jesus called His disciples the salt of the earth, He was describing their preserving function in a world subject to decay. The person in recovery who finds genuine covenant relationship with God becomes a preserving presence — not immune to the decay around them, but a force that slows it rather than accelerates it.

The Vine And Branches As Abiding And Dependency

“I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.” — John 15:5 KJV The branch does not generate its own sap. It receives what the vine provides and produces fruit as a result of the receiving. The branch that is cut off does not produce less fruit — it produces none. And it begins to die. The person who has been living cut off from the source has been producing the fruit of a branch without a vine: nothing sustainable, nothing genuine, nothing that lasts past the next season. The reconnection to the vine is not improvement — it is restoration of the relationship that made fruitfulness possible.

Leaven As Hidden Influence

“The kingdom of heaven is like unto leaven, which a woman took, and hid in three measures of meal, till the whole was leavened.” — Matthew 13:33 KJV Leaven works invisibly. You cannot see it working. But it changes everything it touches, thoroughly and irreversibly. In Galatians, Paul uses leaven as the image of small compromise that infiltrates the whole: "a little leaven leaveneth the whole lump." The one reservation in the Step 3 surrender. The one resentment left unaddressed in Step 4. The one omission in Step 5. What seems small is not small — it is leaven, working through everything it has access to.

The Lamp And Light As Understanding And Conscience

“The spirit of man is the candle of the LORD, searching all the inward parts of the belly.” — Proverbs 20:27 KJV The lamp or candle in Scripture is often the capacity for honest self-examination — the spirit of man that God uses to illuminate what is hidden in the inner life. The inventory of Step 4 is a lit lamp taken into rooms that have been kept dark. What has lived in the dark has not ceased to exist because it was dark. The light reveals it. And what is revealed can be addressed. THE FOUNDATION AS WHAT THE LIFE IS BUILT ON “And the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house; and it fell not: for it was founded upon a rock.” — Matthew 7:25 KJV The storm does not reveal the quality of the house — it reveals the quality of the foundation. Two houses can look identical through years of good weather. The storm is what shows which one was built on sand. The life in active addiction has often produced a presentable surface over a sand foundation. The steps are not the house — they are the process of discovering what the foundation actually is, removing the sand, and allowing God to lay what will hold through the storms that come.

The Yoke As Governing Authority

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me.” — Matthew 11:28-29 KJV A yoke is a governing instrument. It determines the direction, pace, and purpose of what wears it. The person in active addiction wears a yoke — it is just not the yoke Jesus is describing. The addiction is the yoke. The shame is the yoke. The fear is the yoke. The labour and heaviness Jesus speaks to is the labour of carrying a governing authority that was never designed for the person wearing it. The exchange He offers is not freedom from governance — it is freedom from the wrong governor.

The Wedding Garment As Prepared Readiness

“And when the king came in to see the guests, he saw there a man which had not on a wedding garment: And he saith unto him, Friend, how camest thou in hither not having a wedding garment? And he was speechless.” — Matthew 22:11-12 KJV The man was at the wedding — in the right place, present at the right event. But he was not wearing what the event required. Presence at the right location is not the same as being prepared for what happens there. Many people in recovery are in the right place. They are attending. They are present. The question the curriculum raises is whether they are wearing what the event requires — the garment of genuine inward transformation rather than the clothing of appearance. The man was speechless because he had no answer. He knew.

The Fool'S Confession

“The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God.” — Psalm 14:1 KJV The fool is not necessarily an atheist. He is a person who lives as though God is not the governing reality of their life — who has built a life that, in practice, requires no God to function. Every decision made without reference to God. Every strategy for managing the problem developed independently. Every plan for recovery built on what the self can produce. Most people who arrive here have lived as the practical fool without intending to. Not because they rejected God consciously but because the pull of the flesh is strong enough that it gradually became the operating system — the thing that actually made decisions — even while the belief remained verbal. The honest work begins here: not with God somewhere far off requiring you to earn your way back, but with the recognition that He has been present the whole time — and that the life built without Him has produced exactly what a life built without Him produces.

No Man Can Tame The Tongue

“For every kind of beasts, and of birds, and of serpents, and of things in the sea, is tamed, and hath been tamed of mankind: But the tongue can no man tame; it is an unruly evil, full of deadly poison.” — James 3:7-8 KJV If the tongue — the smallest member — cannot be tamed by human effort, what makes anyone believe the deeper appetites can be? The habits of decades. The patterns inherited from childhood. The ruts worn so deep that the wheel goes there automatically without a decision being made. James is not writing to unusual people. He is writing to people who have already received the message of the gospel and are walking in the life it produces. And even for them, the tongue is the remaining evidence that human effort has a ceiling. This is not said to discourage. It is said to diagnose accurately. The ceiling on human effort is not the end of the story. It is the beginning of the real one — the one where what is impossible for man becomes possible for God.

The Leopard And The Spots

“Can the Ethiopian change his skin, or the leopard his spots? then may ye also do good, that are accustomed to do evil.” — Jeremiah 13:23 KJV The question is rhetorical. No. The person accustomed to evil cannot simply decide to do good. The pattern runs too deep. It is not a matter of information or intention — the person who knows what is right and wants to do what is right still finds themselves doing what they have always done. But the question Jeremiah asks is not the last word. The God who cannot change the leopard's spots is not the question. The question is what the God who made the leopard can do. And that question has an answer that runs from Genesis to Revelation: He makes all things new. He gives a new heart. He puts a new spirit within. He does what the leopard cannot do for itself. This is the pattern Scripture shows from beginning to end: God initiates, love reaches first, and the human response is always a return to what was already being offered.

Where This Program Leads

This is not a self-improvement program. It is not behavior management. It is not image correction. It is the pursuit of a genuinely transformed life — the kind that begins with honesty about what is actually there, proceeds through the obedience that God has always required, and ends at a destination the person at Step 1 cannot yet see. “Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.” — 2 Corinthians 5:17 KJV New. Not improved. Not managed. New. That is what is available to the person who walks this road honestly. The twelve steps are not the source of that newness — they are the road that leads toward the God who is. This program does not end with better behavior. It ends with a new identity — the identity of a person who has been genuinely transformed at the source, not merely managed at the surface. That is what is available. That is what the road leads toward. The road that follows is not self-improvement. It is the road toward the God who makes all things new — and the steps are the honest preparation for what He alone can do.

Teacher Notes

Foundation Lesson 1 PUBLIC LESSON. This is the first thing a student encounters. The entire tone of this session is set by 1 John 4:19 — we love Him because He first loved us. The program is not presented as a demand but as a response to love already given. Meet every person in the room with that posture before anything else is said.

Covenant Context For The Teacher

This lesson opens at the same place the Bible opens — with a God who initiates. The covenant pattern from Genesis onward is God reaching toward His people, not people earning their way to God. Ground the opening in that pattern. The student who arrives feeling disqualified needs to understand that the God who is being introduced has a long history of reaching people who felt exactly that way.

The Primary Passage — 1 John 4:19

Read it before the session begins and let it govern the room. We love Him because He first loved us. Every person in this room is here because something drew them — whether they name it that way or not. God's love reached them before they reached back. That is the frame for everything that follows.

Galatians 5:19-21 — How To Teach It

Read the full list slowly and without inflection. Do not rush past the middle of the list to get to drunkenness. Let wrath, strife, envy, and heresies land alongside it. The point is not to shame — it is to show the full scope of what the flesh produces. Students often arrive believing their specific issue is worse than others in the room. The list shows them all in the same category under the same diagnosis. AMOS 6:1 — THE PERSON AT EASE IN ZION Name this directly and without apology. Some people in the room came because of a visible crisis. Others came because something drew them and they cannot fully explain why. The person at ease in Zion may not recognize themselves yet. Plant the seed. The curriculum will develop it.

Pastoral Pressure Points

1. "I've tried this before." Respond: the trying you did before — what was it aimed at? If the target was behavior, the ceiling you hit was real. This program is aimed at something the behavior was always a symptom of. 2. "I'm not as bad as some people here." Respond: Galatians 5 has one list. Wrath is in it. Envy is in it. The measurement is not what others in the room have done. The measurement is the character of God. 3. "I don't believe God cares about me specifically." Respond with Genesis 3:9 — the first thing God did after the hiding was call the name. He knew where they were. He called anyway.

Discussion Anchors — Drawn From The Text

1. From Genesis 3:8 — "What have you been hiding? Not from the group — what have you been keeping from honest examination?" 2. From Jeremiah 13:23 — "What pattern in your life has proven to be beyond your own ability to change?" 3. From 1 John 4:19 — "If love came first — before your effort, before your performance — what does that change about why you are here?"

The Road Forward

This lesson names the problem — the flesh, the hiding, the ceiling on human effort. Lesson 2 goes deeper into what drives the flesh at the root. The student who leaves this session with one honest answer to the question "what am I done hiding" is positioned for what comes next.
