Draw nigh to God — sought through prayer and meditation and what the seeking is building toward

The Covenant Context

Draw nigh to God — sought through prayer and meditation and what the seeking is building toward n PUBLIC — Open Access In Step 10 we saw that the examined life must be a daily practice. Step 11 is where that daily practice finds its source — the God who sustains what human discipline alone cannot maintain. Step 11 is the deepening. The first ten steps have cleared the ground, addressed the past, and built the maintenance practice. Now there is only one thing left to name clearly — and it is the thing the entire program has been pointing toward from the first lesson. You are seeking God. Not a practice. Not a discipline. Not a principle or a method. The living God — the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. The God who made you, who sees you, who has been working in you across every difficult step of this program. That God. Sought. Through prayer and meditation. To improve the conscious contact that is not optional but essential to everything recovery requires. Step 11 says it plainly: sought through prayer and meditation to improve our conscious contact with God. The object of the seeking is not a feeling of peace. Not spiritual growth as an abstract goal. Not a healthier mindset. God Himself. Sought. Deliberately. Through two specific disciplines — prayer and meditation — that together constitute the seeking. “And ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart.” — Jeremiah 29:13 KJV With all your heart. That is the condition. Not the occasional prayer. Not the devotional when it is convenient. A seeking that is wholehearted — because a halfhearted seeking of God is no seeking at all. “But without faith it is impossible to please him: for he that cometh to God must believe that he is, and that he is a rewarder of them that diligently seek him.” — Hebrews 11:6 KJV Diligently seek Him. He is a rewarder of the diligent seeker — not the casual one. And what God gives to the diligent seeker is not information about Himself. It is Himself — His presence, His power working in the person from within, the filling of the life that has been emptied of self-sufficiency and made available. The person who arrives at Step 11 having worked Steps 1 through 10 honestly has been seeking God across the whole year of this program — in the painful honesty of Step 4, in the courage of Step 5, in the surrender of Step 7, in the humility of Step 9. Step 11 names what has been happening and makes it explicit, intentional, and daily. GOD IS THE OBJECT — PRAYER AND MEDITATION ARE THE MEANS Prayer is speaking to God — bringing every need, every confession, every decision, every moment of gratitude and desperation directly to the God who listens. Not a ritual. Not a formula. A conversation with a real and present God. Meditation is the listening side — stilling the internal noise that recovery has been stripping away step by step, until you are quiet enough to hear what God speaks back through His Word, through the check in your spirit, through the counsel of people who walk with Him. “Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you.” — James 4:8 KJV The promise is simple and absolute. Draw near to God — not to a practice, not to a feeling, not to the performance of seeking without the substance of it — and He will draw near to you. Step 11 is the drawing near. And what you find when you draw near is not an experience. It is God. “Be still, and know that I am God.” — Psalm 46:10 KJV Be still. The addiction was partly a mechanism for not being still — for filling the silence that God inhabits. Step 11 asks you to stop filling it. To sit in it. And to know who is there. FALSE PEACE VS. TRUE PEACE — WHAT STEP 11 IS ACTUALLY SEEKING Elijah, having just called down fire from heaven, sat under a juniper tree and asked God to take his life. That is where the angel met him — not at Carmel, but at the juniper tree. Not in the triumph, but in the exhaustion. God meets the person where they actually are. Every addiction began as a search for peace. The substance or behavior delivered something that felt like peace — the quieting of anxiety, the numbing of pain, the silencing of the voice that kept saying something is wrong. It was a counterfeit. “Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you.” — John 14:27 KJV Not as the world giveth. The world's peace requires the right circumstances, the right chemistry, the right level of distraction. It is conditional, temporary, and always fragile. When the circumstances change — when the effect wears off — the anxiety returns, louder for having been suppressed. The peace Jesus gives is structurally different. It is not dependent on circumstances. It is not produced by chemistry. It does not require distraction from what is real. “The LORD will give strength unto his people; the LORD will bless his people with peace.” — Psalm 29:11 KJV This is what Step 11 is pursuing in its prayer and meditation — not the management of anxiety but the settled peace of a life genuinely aligned with God. The peace that was counterfeited by the addiction is the same peace that is available from its only real source. Step 11 is the process of learning to go directly to the source rather than through the substitute. Scripture shows two distinct realities that must not be collapsed. The first is the moment the Spirit enters the believer — the New Birth, the point of indwelling, what Acts records consistently from the upper room to Ephesus. The second is the ongoing operations of the Spirit through the believer — what Paul the apostle describes in First Corinthians 12 as the diversities of gifts distributed as the Spirit wills. These are not the same category. Entry precedes operation. The Spirit enters the believer first. The Spirit then operates through the believer in varied gifts for the building of the body. Confusing these two collapses the order that both Acts and Paul keep carefully distinct. The question is not which gifts a person might receive. The question is whether the Spirit has entered — and Scripture shows consistently what that entrance sounds like when it comes. GIFTS GIVEN FOR THE PROFIT OF ALL — NOT FOR PERSONAL DISPLAY Paul wrote this to people in the church at Corinth who had already received the Spirit — describing not what to seek but how what had already been given operates for the benefit of all: “But the manifestation of the Spirit is given to every man to profit withal.” — 1 Corinthians 12:7 KJV Peter wrote this to born-again believers who had received what God promises to the obedient — calling those already born again into the covenant life to deploy outwardly what had been given inwardly: “As every man hath received the gift, even so minister the same one to another, as good stewards of the manifold grace of God.” — 1 Peter 4:10 KJV To profit withal. The manifestation of the Spirit — what the Spirit expresses through the filled and yielded life — is given for the benefit of others. Not for the spiritual development of the person who carries it. Not as evidence of their standing or achievement. For the profit of the body around them. Minister the same one to another. The gift received is immediately directional — it moves toward the people in front of the person who carries it. The gift of wisdom is not stored. The gift of discernment is not accumulated. The gift of faith is not displayed. Each is ministered — given, poured out, applied to the situation of the person who needs it. This is why Step 11's prayer is not only about the person's own growth. It is the seeking of God by a person who is now a vessel for what others need — asking for what the Spirit gives so that what is given can immediately move outward. The Step 11 person who is only seeking for themselves has not fully understood what the manifestation of the Spirit is for. HOW TO PRAY FOR GOD's WILL — NOT YOUR OWN AGENDA “And this is the confidence that we have in him, that, if we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us: And if we know that he hear us, whatsoever we ask, we know that we have the petitions that we desired of him.” — 1 John 5:14-15 KJV John wrote this to born-again believers — not as a blank check for personal desire but as the confidence that belongs specifically to prayer aligned with God's will. The condition is exact: according to His will. The confidence follows the condition. You cannot have the confidence without the alignment. Most people begin prayer as a petition for what they want. There is nothing wrong with bringing needs before God — the Psalms are full of it and Jesus taught us to ask. But the person who has worked these steps honestly has been learning something across twelve steps that changes how they pray: God's direction is better than their preference. God's timing is better than their urgency. God's answer is better than their request. The prayer that is according to His will begins not with the request but with the listening. “Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven.” — Matthew 6:10 KJV Jesus put this in the middle of the prayer He taught — after the acknowledgment of who God is and before the asking for daily needs. Thy will be done. Not: here is what I need, please approve. Thy will be done. The posture precedes the petition. The surrender of the outcome comes before the voicing of the need. This is not passivity. It is the posture of the person who knows that what God purposes is better than what they can see to ask for. “Likewise the Spirit also helpeth our infirmities: for we know not what we should pray for as we ought: but the Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered.” — Romans 8:26 KJV Paul wrote this to born-again believers at Rome — acknowledging what every honest person discovers in prayer: we do not always know what to ask. The need is real but the specific answer is beyond what we can see from where we are. The Spirit intercedes in the gap between what we can articulate and what God knows we need. This is the prayer of a person in Step 11: God, I do not know what I need. I know who You are. I know what You promised. I am presenting myself — and trusting You to intercede where my understanding falls short. THREE SIGNS YOUR PRAYER HAS SHIFTED FROM SELF TO GOD First: you begin to ask for what others need rather than only for what you need. The person whose prayer has shifted from self-seeking to God-seeking finds that intercession for others becomes natural — not obligatory. Second: you become less anxious about the outcome of what you asked for. The person praying according to God's will is not managing the answer. They have presented the matter to the One whose answer is better than their request, and left it there. Third: you begin to recognize when what you were praying for was not actually what you needed — and feel gratitude that God answered differently than you asked. That recognition is the evidence of a maturing prayer life. The prayer of Step 11 is not a performance of spiritual discipline. It is the daily conversation of a person who has discovered that God answers — and that His answer is worth aligning with. THE PROMISE OF THE

Father

“And, behold, I send the promise of my Father upon you: but tarry ye in the city of Jerusalem, until ye be endued with power from on high.” — Luke 24:49 KJV Jesus said this as His final instruction before ascending — not to strangers, not to people who had just heard Him for the first time, but to the disciples who had walked with Him for three years, who had seen the miracles, who believed He was the Christ. And He told them: wait. There is something you have not yet received. There is a promise of the Father that has not yet arrived. Do not go until it comes. What was the promise of the Father that the disciples — believing, following, committed — had not yet received? “And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance.” — Acts 2:4 KJV This is what arrived. This is what they waited for. This is what Peter stood up and identified: "This is that which was spoken by the prophet Joel." The promise of the Father was not a feeling of peace, not a renewed commitment, not a decision renewed in prayer. It was a specific, identifiable arrival — with a sound, with evidence, with a witness that could be seen and heard by everyone present. The curriculum has been walking toward this question since the preface. Now it is time to name it directly: The steps clear the vessel. The vessel is being cleared for something. The something is not the steps themselves. The steps point toward the God who fills what is genuinely surrendered to Him. And the filling He has promised — the promise of the Father that Jesus sent the disciples to wait for — is the Holy Ghost. “For the promise is unto you, and to your children, and to all that are afar off, even as many as the Lord our God shall call.” — Acts 2:39 KJV This promise was not exclusive to the day of Pentecost. Peter said it plainly: it is unto you. And to your children. And to all that are afar off. The same promise. The same arrival. The same evidence. For as many as the Lord our God shall call. Draw nigh to God. This is what He is drawing nigh with. The pattern across the whole of Scripture is that those who drew near to God found that He drew near to them. Not as a formula — as a relationship. The seeking produced the encounter. The encounter produced the sustaining. The sustaining produced everything that followed.

How Much More Will Your Father Give

“If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?” — Luke 11:13 KJV Jesus said this in the context of teaching about persistent prayer — the friend at midnight, the ask-seek-knock sequence of verses 9-10. And then He named what the heavenly Father gives to those who ask with persistence and genuine seeking: the Holy Spirit. Not general blessing. Not vague divine favor. The Holy Spirit — specifically named as the gift the Father gives to those who ask Him. Step 11 is the step of drawing nigh to God — the deepening of the relationship through prayer and the daily Word. And Jesus identified what the Father gives to the person who draws nigh with genuine seeking: Himself, in the form of the Holy Spirit. The drawing nigh of Step 11 is building toward the specific gift that Luke 11:13 names. Ask for it. Not as a formula. As the honest request of a person who has read what the Father promises to give and wants what He is offering. The person who genuinely seeks God finds that the seeking itself is answered. Not always immediately. Not always dramatically. But the pattern of Scripture is clear: draw nigh to God, and He will draw nigh to thee. The daily posture of drawing near is the posture of a person who has understood who they are and who God is. The person sustained by prayer and contact in this step is ready to carry the message. Step 12 is the reason all eleven steps preceded it. There is a provision at the end of this road that does not depend on human effort to sustain it. Every step from 1 through 10 has been preparation — not for a better version of the old life, but for the new birth Jesus described to Nicodemus. The Spirit who enters does not come as one gift among many. He comes as the one who makes all that follows possible.
