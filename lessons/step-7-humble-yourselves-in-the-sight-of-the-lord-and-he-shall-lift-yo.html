Step 7 · Lesson 1 · Free

Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord — and He shall lift you up

The Covenant Context

Throughout the covenant history, the people who received the most from God were consistently the people who came to Him with the least reliance on their own position. Abraham left everything he knew. Moses argued that he was not sufficient. Gideon was the least in his father's house. David was the youngest and was left in the field when Samuel came. The pattern is not coincidence. It is the consistent expression of what James states plainly: God resisteth the proud and giveth grace to the humble. Step 7 is the step that takes what Step 6 prepared and brings it before God with nothing added — no performance, no augmentation, no presentation of the self as deserving of the removal. Just the asking. Step 6 established something important: there are things in the character that human effort named but could not remove. That is not failure — it is the honest prerequisite for what this step asks. Peter's instruction in Acts 2:38 begins with one word: Repent. Then baptism. Then the gift. But the sequence begins with the humble turning — which is exactly what Step 7 is. The person who humbles himself before God and asks for what only God can give is doing precisely what the apostolic record shows happening before the name is applied and the Spirit is received. When Peter stood on the day of Pentecost and told the crowd what to do, the first word he spoke was repent. Not believe harder. Not try again. Repent — and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins. The remission — the sending away — of sin is not accomplished by the decision of Step 3 alone. It is accomplished by the full covenant response: repentance, the name, and the Spirit. This is what the steps have been preparing the road toward.

Humble Yourselves In The Sight Of The Lord

“Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and he shall lift you up.” — James 4:10 KJV James wrote this to born-again believers — people who had received the Spirit and were being instructed in the posture that maintains availability to God's ongoing work. Humble yourselves. The humbling is the action of the person. The lifting is the action of God. The sequence is fixed. The lifting does not precede the humbling. It follows it. Humility in Step 7 is not self-contempt. It is accurate assessment — knowing what the self can produce and what only God can produce, and placing what only God can produce in His hands without managing the delivery. The tax collector in the temple did not lift his eyes toward heaven. He beat his breast and said: God be merciful to me a sinner. Jesus said he went home justified. Humility is not a performance. It is the honest posture of the person who knows what they are.

The Proud And The Humble

“God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble.” — James 4:6 KJV Resisteth. Not ignores. Not passes over. Actively opposes. The proud person — the person who approaches God with what they have already accomplished, with the inventory of their effort and their progress and their willingness — meets resistance. Not because God is harsh but because the proud posture has already placed something between the person and the grace that is being offered. The humble person — the person who comes with nothing to offer but the honest naming of what they need — receives grace. Not because they earned it by being sufficiently humble. Because the posture of genuine humility removes the obstacle that the proud posture erected.

What Humility Is Not

Humility is not the performance of lowliness. The person who has learned that appearing humble produces approval has developed a sophisticated form of pride. True humility is not managed. It is the honest accounting of what the self is and what the self is not — without embellishment in either direction. “For I say, through the grace given unto me, to every man that is among you, not to think of himself more highly than he ought to think; but to think soberly, as God hath dealt to every man the measure of faith.” — Romans 12:3 KJV Paul wrote this to born-again believers in Rome — instructing them in the sober self-assessment that genuine humility produces. Not lower than accurate. Not higher than accurate. Sober. The person in Step 7 does not arrive with a list of what they have accomplished. They arrive with an honest accounting of what they have been unable to produce and a genuine request for the removal of what they are unable to remove on their own.

The Broken And Contrite Heart

“The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.” — Psalm 34:18 KJV Broken does not mean destroyed. It means no longer relying on its own structural integrity. The broken heart is the one that has stopped trusting its own capacity to hold itself together and has placed its weight on what God provides instead. That is the posture of Step 7. The contrite spirit — the crushed, ground-down acknowledgment of what the self has produced — is not the destination. It is the condition that opens the person to what God gives to those who come that way. He is nigh. He saves. Not to the performance of seeking without the substance. To the broken heart that has genuinely arrived. The pattern is consistent across the whole of Scripture: God lifts those who humble themselves. Not those who perform humility — those who actually occupy the low place honestly. The lifting is always in response to the genuine posture, never to the performance of it. THE CRY — WHAT THE SPIRIT PRODUCES WHEN IT ENTERS “And because ye are sons, God hath sent forth the Spirit of his Son into your hearts, crying, Abba, Father.” — Galatians 4:6 KJV Paul wrote this to born-again believers in Galatia — describing what happens when the Spirit enters the heart. A cry. Not a private internal impression. A cry — the Spirit of God within producing a voiced expression of the relationship that has been established. Romans confirms it: "For ye have not received the spirit of bondage again to fear; but ye have received the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father. The Spirit itself beareth witness with our spirit, that we are the children of God." — Romans 8:15-16 KJV The Spirit bears witness. And it does so with a cry — something that comes from within but is not manufactured by the person. Something that arrives with the Spirit's arrival. The person who has received the Spirit of adoption knows the difference between the cry that was produced and the prayer that was composed. One is the work of the self. The other is the work of the Spirit bearing witness. Carry this question through the coming steps: what does it mean that the Spirit bears witness with your spirit? What would that witness look like? What would it sound like? The person who has genuinely asked God to remove what they could not remove has done something they could not undo. They have acknowledged in the most specific possible way what is true: they cannot complete themselves. And God, who is faithful to that kind of honesty, lifts what is brought to Him. The prayer established in this step becomes the ongoing contact of Step 11. What begins here as a request becomes a relationship.
