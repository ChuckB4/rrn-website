Step 3 · Lesson 1 · Free

Not my will but Thine — what it means to actually make the decision

The Covenant Context

The covenant story has always required a decision. God presented the terms and waited for the response. Abraham had to leave Ur before the destination was revealed. Israel had to step into the Jordan before the water parted. The obedience preceded the confirmation in every case. And the specific word God required was always some version of the same thing: not what you planned, not what you arranged, not what your strength can produce — Mine. Step 3 is the moment the person who has admitted powerlessness in Step 1 and encountered the able God in Step 2 makes the actual decision. Not the intention to decide eventually. Not the agreement that surrender is a good idea in principle. The decision. Today. With this specific will. This specific life. “Not my will, but thine, be done.” — Luke 22:42 KJV Jesus prayed this in the Gospels, in the garden of Gethsemane, the night before the cross — the moment the full weight of what the new covenant would require landed on the One who was making it. The prayer was not the prayer of someone for whom surrender was easy. It was the prayer of someone who felt the full weight of what was being asked and surrendered anyway. That is the prayer of Step 3. In Step 2 we came to believe that restoration was possible — but belief alone, as we saw, is not enough to produce it. Step 3 is where belief becomes a decision. The surrender of Step 3 is a transfer of authority. It is the decision to live under God's governance rather than the self's. There is a word for this in the Scripture — calling on the name of the Lord. Not the recitation of a formula, but the genuine turning of the person toward the God who has made Himself known by name and promised to answer those who call on Him genuinely. The surrender of Step 3 is the outward form of what the Scripture calls repentance at the level of will — not remorse, but the actual turning of the governance of the life toward God. Remorse stays in the far country. Repentance arises, and comes home.

What The Decision Actually Is

The decision of Step 3 is not the decision to feel differently about God. It is not the decision to attend more consistently or try harder or adopt better practices. It is the transfer of authority — the actual, specific surrender of the will that has been running the operation, and the placement of that will under the governance of the God who is able. “I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service.” — Romans 12:1 KJV Paul wrote this to believers in Rome who had already entered the covenant life — describing the ongoing act of surrender that the life in God requires. A living sacrifice. The sacrifice is not made once and complete. It is the daily, ongoing presentation of what is genuinely available to the One who has been given the governance. Step 3 is the first and foundational presentation. What follows builds on it daily. The decision of Step 3 is made by a person who has not yet been changed — who is still in the condition that made Step 1 necessary. That is exactly the right person to make it.

What Is Being Surrendered

The will. Specifically, the will — the part of the person that has been making decisions about what to do, what to pursue, what to protect, what to avoid. The will is not bad. It is a good thing given for the right purpose. The problem is not that the person has a will. The problem is whose governance the will has been under. The surrender of Step 3 is not the destruction of the will. It is the reorientation of it — from self-governance to the governance of the God who is able to direct it where it was designed to go.

The Road Forward

The pattern across the covenant is clear: God does not take what has not been given. Surrender is the door. It has always been the door. Every transformation recorded in Scripture begins with a person giving something over that they had been holding. The decision has been named and its content identified. Lesson 2 addresses what it looks like to walk in that decision daily — how the Word lights the road and what obedience looks like as a daily practice rather than a single event. SURRENDERED TO THE NAME THAT

Governs

“I have even called thee by thy name: I have surnamed thee, though thou hast not known me.” — Isaiah 45:4 KJV God spoke this to Cyrus — a Persian king who did not know the God of Israel — and named him before his work had begun. God does more than call by name. He surnames. A surname is the family name — the identity that is carried from one generation to the next, the mark of whose household you belong to. The decision of Step 3 — not my will but Thine — is the beginning of a different governance. Not only the surrender of daily choices but the deeper surrender of the name under which the life is conducted. The self's name, the self's identity, the self's plan for what the life is for — all of it placed under the governance of the One who has always had a name, who has always placed that name upon His people, and who has always blessed what was surrendered to bear it. As the steps proceed, the significance of whose name you bear will become increasingly clear. For now: the surrender of Step 3 is the beginning of a life conducted under a different authority than the self's authority. And that authority has a name. THE POOL OF SILOAM — OBEDIENCE BEFORE THE RESULT APPEARS Jesus put clay on the blind man's eyes and said: go, wash in the pool of Siloam. The man did not ask how the clay would help. He did not negotiate the method. He did not wait until he understood the mechanism. He went. “He went his way therefore, and washed, and came seeing.” — John 9:7 KJV The healing was not in the understanding. It was in the going. The man could not see where he was going — he was blind. He walked by faith in the instruction he had received, toward the pool he was told to go to, and he came back seeing. Step 3 — the decision to turn the will and the life over to the care of God — is the pool of Siloam moment of the program. The person at Step 3 does not yet have the sight that Steps 4 through 12 will develop. They are making a decision whose full meaning will only become clear after the decision has been kept. The instruction is: go. Surrender. Walk toward the pool you cannot yet see clearly. The sight comes after the washing, not before. The decision of Step 3 does not require you to understand how it will produce what it promises. It requires you to go. The decision of Step 3, when it is real, changes what the person is from that moment forward. Not because they feel different — because they have placed something they were governing under a different governance. That transfer is the beginning of a life that works differently than the one that brought them here. The person who has genuinely surrendered does not fear what the inventory will find. What belongs to God can be looked at honestly.
