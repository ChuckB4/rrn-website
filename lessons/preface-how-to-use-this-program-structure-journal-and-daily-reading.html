Preface · Lesson 4 · Free

How to use this program — structure, journal, and daily reading

The Covenant Context

Every covenant God has made has come with instructions — not to burden His people but to show them how to walk in what He has provided. The Law was not punishment. It was the gift of knowing how to live as the people of God. The commands of the New Testament are not demands imposed on reluctant servants — they are the instructions of a Father who knows the road and wants His children to walk it well. This program has structure. The structure is not the program — it is the container that holds the program while the real work happens. The daily reading, the journal, the scripture memory, the quizzes, the mentor relationship — none of these are the transformation. They are the conditions that make the transformation possible. God does the work. These disciplines create the daily availability for that work. Give the structure the respect it deserves — not because it will save you, but because the God who is doing the saving has asked you to show up consistently and He meets consistency with what only He can give.

The Daily Reading Plan

Each lesson comes with seven daily readings — one for each day of the week. These are not optional supplements to the lesson. They are the lesson continuing into the daily life. The Sunday teaching reaches the mind. The seven daily readings reach the habit. Read the assigned passage. Write a response to the reflection prompt. Keep it honest. The journal is not an assignment to be graded — it is a conversation with God about what the passage is showing you today. The Word is in the morning before the phone. Not after the news. Not after email. Not when time permits. Before. The first act of the waking day is the declaration that what God says governs what the day will hold — before the day has made its demands, before the flesh has been given its instructions, before the world has filled the attention that belongs to God first. This is not a rule. It is the practical expression of what the preface has been establishing: He is first. Everything else comes after.

The Journal

Write honestly. Not what sounds appropriately spiritual. Not what a mentor should see. What is actually happening — what the passage showed you, what it unsettled, what it confirmed, what you want to avoid. God already knows what is in the heart. The journal is not for His benefit. It is for yours — the practice of naming what is actually true, which is the skill the entire program is asking you to develop. The person who journals honestly recovers at a different rate than the person who journals for appearance. The difference is not the writing. It is the honesty.

Scripture Memory

The verses are not in the curriculum for decoration. They are there because the Word hidden in the heart is the Word that functions under pressure. When the temptation comes there will not be time to look it up. The person who has carried Psalm 139:23 in their chest reaches for it in the moment of need without thinking. The person who has it on their phone reaches for their phone — and the phone leads somewhere else. Memorize the verse this week. Write it until you can say it without looking. Then say it out loud. The words in the mouth have a different weight than the words in the eye.

Quizzes And Step Exams

The quizzes test whether the lesson has been understood — not performed. A student who passes without understanding has only performed. A student who retakes and passes has done exactly what the program intends: the material was received, processed, and understood. The passing score for lesson quizzes is 70%. Step exams require 80%. Graduation requires 85%. These are not arbitrary — they correspond to the depth of understanding each phase of the program requires. You are not being tested. You are being formed.

The Mentor Connection

The mentor is not the program. The mentor is the person further along the road who has agreed to walk beside you for the year. They are not there to approve or disapprove of your answers. They are there because the confession of Step 5, the accountability of Step 10, and the posture of Step 11 all require another human being present. The person who tries to work the steps without a mentor is trying to do in private what the steps were designed to do in relationship. Some things do not happen in isolation. Name it when you feel resistance to the mentor relationship — because that resistance is usually protecting something that the program needs to reach.

A Note On The Language Of This Curriculum

The Scripture this program uses is the King James Bible — and the King James Bible never uses the word "to morrow." It says "to morrow" or "on the morrow." This is not an accident. The fool of Proverbs 27:1 says "Boast not thyself of to morrow; for thou knowest not what a day may bring forth." James 4:13-14 says: "Go to now, ye that say, To day or to morrow we will go into such a city..." — and then identifies the person who speaks that way as the one who does not know what shall be on the morrow. This curriculum follows the same language of the Word: it does not speak of "to morrow" as a settled thing. The road is walked today. Today's obedience. Today's inventory. Today's surrender. The to morrow belongs to God.

One Week At A Time

The steps that feel impossible from the beginning of the program become possible one week at a time. Do not read Step 9 at Step 2 and decide it is more than you can do. You are not at Step 9. You are at Step 2. Step 9 will arrive when it is supposed to arrive — after Steps 3 through 8 have done what they do. Trust the process because the process is designed by the same God who is doing the work. THE BURNING BUSH — GOD APPEARS IN THE BACK SIDE OF THE DESERT “And the angel of the LORD appeared unto him in a flame of fire out of the midst of a bush: and he looked, and, behold, the bush burned with fire, and the bush was not consumed.” — Exodus 3:2 KJV Moses was in the back side of the desert. Not at the height of his influence. Not when his circumstances had improved. At eighty years old, tending sheep that were not his, in a wilderness he had been in for forty years — having fled from the life he was supposed to lead. That is where God appeared. Not in the temple. Not at the peak of the career. In the back side of the desert. In the ordinary work of the most unremarkable day. The person who believes God requires a better version of them before He shows up has the wrong picture. He appears in the back side of the desert. In the wilderness that resulted from the years of running. In the ordinary work of an ordinary day. The bush is burning right now. In this room. While you are tending these sheep. HE ALREADY KNOWS — THE CONFESSION IS NOT NEWS TO GOD “For there is not a word in my tongue, but, lo, O LORD, thou knowest it altogether.” — Psalm 139:4 KJV Every word before it is spoken. Every thought before it forms. Every hiding place before you entered it. The confession of Step 5 is not informing God of something He has not seen. He was present for all of it — not approving, but present. Knowing. And having known it completely, He has not turned away. This is what makes honesty possible. You are not risking His discovery. He already knows. The confession is the person finally agreeing with what God has always seen — stepping out from behind the cover and standing in the open before the One who already knew. That is not frightening. That is relief.

The Covenant Name — A Seed

From the beginning of the covenant, God has placed His name on His people as the identifying mark of belonging. When He instructed the priests to bless Israel, He said: "And they shall put my name upon the children of Israel; and I will bless them." — Numbers 6:27 KJV The name was not ceremonial decoration. It was identity, authority, and belonging — placed on a person by the One who had the right to name them. Throughout the covenant history, being "called by His name" was the mark of the person who belonged to Him. The curriculum that follows will trace how this naming principle develops across the covenant — from the old covenant where God named a place, to the new covenant where He names a people. For now, carry this single question: what does it mean to bear the name of the One who made you?

How To Read The Bible This Program Uses

God does not change. “For I am the LORD, I change not.” — Malachi 3:6 KJV But the way God has dealt with His people has progressively changed across time — the way a parent deals with a child differently as that child grows from infancy through adolescence into maturity. The character of the parent is the same. The terms of the relationship develop as the relationship deepens and the child becomes capable of more. This is called covenant. God has made and fulfilled a series of covenants across human history — each one building upon what preceded it, each one pointing further toward what was coming. Understanding this is not advanced theology. It is essential to reading the Bible correctly. And every lesson in this curriculum uses the Bible. THE OLD TESTAMENT — PROMISE AND PROPHECY POINTING FORWARD The Law, the Psalms, and the Prophets were given to a specific people at a specific time. They reveal the character of God, the nature of sin, the pattern of human failure, and the promise of what God intended to do about all of it. Every page of the Old Testament is pointing forward to something not yet arrived. THE GOSPELS — THE ARRIVAL OF THE PROMISED ONE Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John record the life, ministry, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. They are written in the New Testament — but the events they describe take place before the new covenant is in full effect. A testament does not become operative until after the death of the one who makes it. “For a testament is of force after men are dead: otherwise it is of no strength at all while the testator liveth.” — Hebrews 9:17 KJV Everything Jesus said and did in the Gospels was being spoken to people still operating under the old covenant, pointing toward the new one His death would establish. The Gospels are the bridge. The new covenant becomes operative after the cross. THE BOOK OF ACTS — THE NEW COVENANT IN OPERATION Acts is the only book in the Bible where you can watch the new covenant teaching of the Gospels become lived experience. It is the record of what actually happened when people responded to the message of what Christ had accomplished. What did genuine new birth look like? What did obedience produce? What did God do in response to the people who heard and obeyed? When Acts is read carefully, the pattern is consistent and testable. This is why the curriculum returns to Acts — particularly Acts 2, Acts 8, Acts 9, Acts 10, and Acts 19 — as the place where doctrine meets reality. Not what people believed in theory. What actually happened when they obeyed. And it happened the same way — across different people, different locations, different backgrounds — every time it is recorded.

The Epistles — Letters Written To Born-Again Believers

Romans, Corinthians, Galatians, Ephesians, and the rest of the letters were written by Paul, Peter, James, John, and Jude to people who had already entered through the obedience Acts records. When the epistles describe the Spirit bearing witness, the gifts operating, the daily walk of the filled life — they are describing the experience of people who had already responded to the message. The entry point is Acts. The ongoing life is what the epistles address. The epistles do not replace what Acts established — they build upon it. REVELATION — FROM THE PERSPECTIVE OF WHAT IS COMING The final book speaks from the vantage point of what God will ultimately accomplish — the completion of every covenant, the final restoration of all things, the permanent dwelling of God with His people. It is read in light of everything that preceded it.

Scripture Interprets Scripture — The Authority Of Acts

“All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness.” — 2 Timothy 3:16 KJV This declaration leaves no room for selective application. All Scripture. Not the portions that are comfortable. Not the portions that confirm what was already believed before the text was opened. All of it — profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction. The word reproof here means to expose what is false, to bring what is hidden into the light of what is true. The Scripture is not only a source of comfort — it is the instrument by which false doctrine is identified and truth is established. Therefore this curriculum affirms without reservation that the book of Acts is not merely a historical record. It is an inspired and authoritative witness of how the message was first proclaimed and consistently received. The events it records are not isolated or random — they are repeated patterns across different people, different locations, and different circumstances, presented without correction, contradiction, or revision anywhere else in Scripture. The letters of the New Testament were written to audiences who had already responded to the message. These writings provide depth, instruction, and explanation for established believers — but they do not erase or redefine the foundational response already demonstrated in Acts. Silence in the letters does not negate what has been clearly and repeatedly established in the record. Sound reading of Scripture requires that Scripture interpret Scripture — that clear examples inform understanding, and that consistent patterns carry the weight they deserve. Where the Word of God reveals a response that is repeated, preserved, and never corrected, it must be received as both descriptive and instructive. Any approach that accepts the outcomes of the early church while dismissing the pattern that produced them introduces an inconsistency that the text itself does not support. It does so not on the basis of what the Scripture says but on assumptions imposed upon it. This curriculum does not make that selection. Acts stands as a doctrinally relevant and authoritative record — providing not only the history of the early church but the God-ordained pattern of response to the message. Clearly revealed. Consistently demonstrated. Fully aligned with the whole of Scripture. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR EVERY LESSON IN THIS CURRICULUM When a lesson uses an Old Testament passage it is pointing to a principle or pattern that runs through all of Scripture. When a Gospel passage is cited it is showing what Jesus established and promised. When Acts is referenced it is showing what obedience to that promise produced in real people. When an epistle is quoted it is describing the life of people who have already entered through obedience. This framework governs how every scripture is being used. Read it with this understanding and what might otherwise seem like disconnected verses from different parts of the Bible will be seen for what they actually are: one God, one story, one progressive covenant — pointing from the beginning toward the same destination. “There is one body, and one Spirit, even as ye are called in one hope of your calling; One Lord, one faith, one baptism, One God and Father of all, who is above all, and through all, and in you all.” — Ephesians 4:4-6 KJV
