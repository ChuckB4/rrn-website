Step 8 · Lesson 1 · Free

If thou bring thy gift to the altar — the list of all persons harmed and the willing heart

The Covenant Context

When Zacchaeus came down from the sycamore tree and received Jesus into his house, he did not wait to be prompted about restitution. The encounter with genuine grace produced an immediate outward movement: "Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold." The internal work had reached the relational field. That is Step 8. Step 8 is not the amends. Step 8 is the list and the willing heart. Both must be present before Step 9 can begin. In Step 7 we arrived at the honest admission that some things require more than human effort — they require what only God can provide. Step 8 is where the changed person begins to address what the old life left behind. The person who bears God's name — who has been surnamed by God — carries that identity into every relationship they address. The amends of Step 8 is not merely making things right. It is the action of a person who is now living under a different name and therefore acting differently than the person who made the original harm. The amends list is the ledger of genuine repentance made concrete in relationship. Repentance that does not move toward those who were harmed has stopped short of what repentance actually is. Step 8 is where the turn of the heart produces the list that Step 9 will walk.

The List

“Therefore if thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother hath ought against thee; Leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.” — Matthew 5:23-24 KJV The list already exists. It was written in Step 4 — the harms column, the names, the specific things that happened. Step 8 takes that list and reviews it through a single lens: who has something against me, and am I willing to go? Review every name. Add any that the writing of Step 4 did not surface but that the journey through Steps 5, 6, and 7 has revealed. The list is not an indictment — it is the preparation for reconciliation. The goal of every name on the list is not punishment of the self but the restoration of what was damaged. Zacchaeus was small of stature and up a tree — literally elevated above the crowd, disconnected from the level ground of honest encounter. Jesus stopped beneath that tree and called him down by name.

The Willing Heart — Where Most People Stall

The list is the easier part. The willing heart is where Step 8 actually does its work. For most people there will be names on the list toward which the willingness does not come naturally. The harm was mutual. The resentment is still present. The person is unreachable, deceased, or in a situation where contact would cause further harm. “And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you.” — Ephesians 4:32 KJV Paul wrote this to born-again believers in Ephesus — describing the character of the person walking in the Spirit. The willingness for reconciliation is not generated by the self. It is the fruit of what the Spirit produces in the person who has genuinely received the forgiveness that Ephesians 4:32 describes. The person who has been forgiven much is capable of forgiving much. The inventory of what has been forgiven in Steps 4 and 5 is the ground from which the willingness for Step 8 grows. For every name toward which willingness does not come: bring it to the Step 7 petition. Ask God for the willingness. The willingness is the fruit of the Spirit — it can be asked for and received before the amends is made.

Leviticus 6 — The Restitution Principle

“Then it shall be, because he hath sinned, and is guilty, that he shall restore that which he took violently away, or the thing which he hath deceitfully gotten, or that which was delivered him to keep, or the lost thing which he found, Or all that about which he hath sworn falsely; he shall even restore it in the principal, and shall add the fifth part more thereto.” — Leviticus 6:4-5 KJV The principle of restitution runs from the Law through the Gospels — Zacchaeus restoring fourfold, Jesus requiring reconciliation before the gift at the altar. Where restitution is possible it is part of the willing heart. What was taken should be returned if possible. What was damaged should be repaired if possible. The willing heart includes the willingness to restore, not only to apologize.

The Road Forward

Scripture shows that the changed life produces changed action. This is not the root — it is the fruit. The amends list is not the source of the transformation. It is the evidence of it. What God has genuinely changed will move toward what it previously damaged. The list is complete. The heart is willing — or willing to become willing through the Step 7 petition. Step 9 is the going. THE NAME UNDER WHICH THE AMENDS IS MADE “And whatsoever ye do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God and the Father by him.” — Colossians 3:17 KJV Paul wrote this to born-again believers in Colosse — giving the governing principle for all action: whatsoever you do, in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus. The name governs the action. Not only the formal religious action — every word, every deed. The amends conversation is a word and a deed. It is done in a name. The name under which the amends is made determines its quality and its source. The amends made from guilt is made in a different name than the amends made from genuine love of God and neighbor. The amends made to repair the self's reputation is made in a different name than the amends made in obedience to the God who requires reconciliation before the gift at the altar. Do the amends in His name. Not yours.

Called By His Name — The Covenant Mark

“If my people, which are called by my name, shall humble themselves, and pray, and seek my face, and turn from their wicked ways; then will I hear from heaven, and will forgive their sin, and will heal their land.” — 2 Chronicles 7:14 KJV My people — called by my name. The covenant mark in the old covenant was the name of God placed on a people. To be called by His name was to carry His identity, to be distinguished from the surrounding world, to bear the mark of belonging to Him rather than to the world. The amends of Steps 8 and 9 is the practical expression of what it means to live as a person called by His name. The name is not only an identity marker — it is a covenant obligation. To bear the name of God is to live in a way that honors what the name means. The reconciliation that Steps 8 and 9 require is the outworking of the covenant obligation that comes with bearing His name. The curriculum will continue to trace where the name of God in the new covenant is placed — and what it means for the person on whom it rests. For now: what does it mean that you are called by His name — and what does that name require of you in the amends you are about to make? The list is not a punishment. It is the record of a person who has been genuinely changed and is now taking inventory of what the old life owed. The identity that produces the list is already different from the identity that created the debts. The list matters. The willingness matters more. Step 9 is where the willingness becomes action — and where the relational ground of the old life begins to be cleared.
