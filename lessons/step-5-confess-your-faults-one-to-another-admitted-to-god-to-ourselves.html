Step 5 · Lesson 1 · Free

Confess your faults one to another — admitted to God, to ourselves, and to another human being

The Covenant Context

The covenant pattern of confession runs from the tabernacle to the New Testament. Under the old covenant, the priest laid the sins of the people on the head of the scapegoat — the act of transference required the naming, the acknowledgment, the public placement of what had been carried. The pattern was never private management. It was always the bringing of what was hidden into the presence of God. James 5:16 is written in the New Testament, to people already in the covenant community, describing the ongoing practice of the life that has genuinely entered it. The confession is mutual — one to another, not only upward. The prayer is mutual. And the healing follows. The healing. Not the feeling better. The healing — the actual addressing of what has been brought into the light. “Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much.” — James 5:16 KJV The step that the inventory prepared for. The step that brings what was written into the presence of another person. Admitted — to God, to ourselves through the inventory, and now to another human being. The triple admission is the completion of the Step 4 honesty extended into relationship. In Step 4 we looked honestly at what the unmanageable life had actually produced — not to condemn, but to name clearly what needed to change. Step 5 brings it into the light. Acts 22:16 records the instruction given to Paul after his encounter on the Damascus road. He had believed. He had seen. He had been fasting and praying for three days. And still Ananias said: "Arise, and be baptized, and wash away thy sins, calling on the name of the Lord." The calling on the name and the washing away of sins are one action described two ways. This is worth noticing. There is a reason the Scripture connects confession and healing in the same sentence. What is confessed before God and another person is not merely acknowledged — it is brought into the light where repentance can complete its work. The confession of Step 5 is repentance made specific.

What The Text Shows

James wrote confess your faults — not your achievements, not your spiritual insights, your faults. The specific things that were wrong. The patterns the inventory revealed. The harms that were done. In the presence of another person who is also in the community of those who have received what God promises. And pray one for another. The confession is received in the context of prayer — not in the context of judgment, not in the context of advice, not in the context of assessment. The other person receives the confession and brings it to God in prayer. That is the posture Step 5 requires from both the person confessing and the person receiving. That ye may be healed. The healing is the promised result of the confession made in this context. Not the result of having told someone — but of having brought what was hidden into the light in the context of genuine covenant community. The woman at the well had been through five marriages and was living with a sixth man when Jesus met her. He did not require her history to be different. He offered living water exactly where she was.

Why Another Person Is Required

The confession to God alone — without the human witness — is real. But the experience of being known by another person and still received is a different kind of healing than the private prayer produces. The person who has confessed everything to God alone has done something real. They have not yet experienced being known by a human witness and received without rejection. That experience is what the human heart needs — and what Step 5 provides. “For where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them.” — Matthew 18:20 KJV Jesus said this in the Gospels — describing the specific presence available in the gathered relationship. The presence of God in the Step 5 confession is not a metaphor. It is the promised reality of the covenant community gathered in His name around the specific, honest, brought-into-the-light disclosure of what was in the inventory.

The Road Forward

The pattern James records — confess your faults one to another and pray one for another, that ye may be healed — is not a suggestion. It is the God-ordained mechanics of how healing actually travels. It moves through relationship, not in isolation. The nature and purpose of the confession have been established. Lesson 2 addresses what specifically happens when the confession is made — and what it produces in the person and the relationship. THE WITNESS “It is the Spirit that beareth witness, because the Spirit is truth.” — 1 John 5:6 KJV John wrote this to born-again believers — pointing to the Spirit as the One who bears witness to what is true. The confession of Step 5 is not only the human act of speaking what has been hidden. It is the moment of alignment between what the person says and what the Spirit has always known to be true. The Spirit bears witness to the honest confession — not as an outside observer but as the inner witness that confirms: this is true, this is what was there, this is the honest accounting. A witness is not a voice alone. A witness is the confirmation of truth by one who was present. The Spirit was present for all of it — the years, the hiding, the inventory entries, the pattern. When the honest confession is made, the Spirit who was present bears witness that it is true. And the witness that matters — the witness that produces the settled knowing — is not the self's own voice. It is the voice that confirms from outside the self. That is the witness of Step 5.

Specificity — Vagueness Is Where Avoidance Lives

One of the most important moments in Step 5 is when vague language becomes specific. Not "I had some problems" but naming exactly what happened and to whom. The vague version protects the person from the full weight of what is true. The specific version is what actually produces movement. Every sponsor who has sat with someone working Step 5 knows the moment when the inventory stops being a summary and becomes a name, a date, a specific act — and that is the moment something actually shifts. The same precision runs through the covenant. In the entire history of God's dealings with His people, He progressively revealed more of Himself as their needs required it. When Abraham needed provision, He revealed Himself as Jehovah-jireh. When Israel needed healing, He was Jehovah-rapha. When they needed victory, Jehovah-nissi. Sixteen compound names across the Old Testament — each one revealing a specific aspect, none of them revealing the saving name. The saving name was withheld. It was secret. When Jacob wrestled with the Angel of the LORD and asked for His name, the Angel would not tell him. What Jacob received instead was the thing wrestling always produces when it is done honestly — a new name for himself. The specificity of confession in Step 5 is its own form of wrestling: you hold on through the night, you come out limping, and you come out named differently. When Manoah asked the same question, the answer was: "Why askest thou thus after my name, seeing it is secret?" — Judges 13:18 KJV . The name was coming. The time had not arrived. “And thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins.” — Matthew 1:21 KJV When the fullness of time came, the name was given. Not a title. Not a category. Not Father, Son, and Holy Ghost as three separate names — one name. Jesus. The Hebrew roots reach back to Jehoshua — Jehovah and Yasha — meaning Jehovah is Salvation. Every compound name from the Old Testament collapses into this one: the Jehovah who provides, heals, gives victory, brings peace — revealed at last in the name that saves. The specificity the confession of Step 5 requires is the same specificity the covenant requires. Not a vague reaching toward God. The specific name. "Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved." — Acts 4:12 KJV Apply the discipline of Step 5 here: what name are you calling on? The person who has completed Step 5 honestly is no longer carrying alone what was always too heavy to carry alone. The secrecy is broken. The isolation that fed the bondage has been interrupted. That is not a small thing. It is the condition the next steps require. Naming it was the first movement. What comes next requires a different kind of willingness — not just to see it, but to let it go.
