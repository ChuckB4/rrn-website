Go ye into all the world — self-examination first, proving your faith before you carry anything

The Covenant Context

Go ye into all the world — self-examination first, proving your faith before you carry anything n PUBLIC — Open Access In Step 1 we admitted we were powerless. What we carry now is the evidence that powerlessness was not the end of the story. “And being made perfect, he became the author of eternal salvation unto all them that obey him.” — Hebrews 5:9 KJV Step 12 begins not with a declaration but with a command. Paul wrote this command to people who had entered the faith through genuine obedience — calling them to verify that what they entered was real, not to perform the examination as a path to entering: “Examine yourselves, whether ye be in the faith; prove your own selves. Know ye not your own selves, how that Jesus Christ is in you, except ye be reprobates?” — 2 Corinthians 13:5 KJV Examine yourselves. Not examine others. Not wait for others to examine you. Yourselves. Whether ye be in the faith — not whether you attended the program, not whether you completed the steps. Whether you are in the faith. Actually in it. The real thing, not the presentation of it. The word prove is the same word used for testing metal — the application of heat to reveal what is genuinely gold and what is surface coating that burns away. The examination Paul commands is that kind of proving. Not comfortable. Not ceremonial. The heat of honest self-assessment revealing what is actually there. Before you carry anything to anyone else — this examination comes first.

What It Means To Be In The Faith

Being in the faith is not the same as having been through a program. It is not the same as being able to articulate the twelve steps. It is not the same as knowing the right language, having the right relationships, or having completed the right assignments. Being in the faith means the God of Scripture — the specific God who authored eternal salvation through the obedience of His Son — has become the operating reality of your daily life. Not the God you reference. The God who governs. “But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.” — James 1:22 KJV Hearers only — deceiving themselves. The person who has heard twelve steps of teaching without the hearing producing the doing has been in the room without being in the faith. The examination of Step 12 asks: is what was heard producing what it was designed to produce — or is the hearing producing the deception that the The disciples were in a locked room when the risen Christ appeared. Not on a mountaintop. Not in the temple. In a locked room, afraid. He came through the walls. The commission came before the fear was gone. hearing is sufficient? EVIDENCE VS. EMOTION Emotion says the program moved me. Evidence says my life is moving differently. Emotion says I felt the presence of God in the Step 5 confession. Evidence says the patterns that drove the addiction are being replaced by the fruit of the Spirit in the daily life. Emotion says I believe I have been changed. Evidence says the people closest to me — the ones who knew the Step 1 version — are witnessing something different in the Step 12 version. The examination of 2 Corinthians 13:5 is not about the emotional experience of recovery. It is about the evidence. The fruit. The daily life that cannot be manufactured by program completion alone. “Ye shall know them by their fruits.” — Matthew 7:16 KJV The tree is known by what it produces — not by what it claims to be, not by what program it completed, not by how long it has been in the room. By the fruit. The examination asks: what fruit is this life producing? And is that fruit genuine — growing from the Spirit's work in a yielded life — or is it the performance of fruit that looks right for the context?

Identifying Hidden Compromise

The examination must go past the surface. “The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it?” — Jeremiah 17:9 KJV The same heart that ran the addiction is capable of running a recovery presentation that looks like transformation. It is capable of maintaining the language, the attendance, the visible disciplines — while concealing the area that was never fully surrendered. Hidden compromise is the defect that was identified in Step 4 and not fully given in Steps 6 and 7. The relationship that was not included in the Step 8 list. The amends that was performed rather than made. The daily seeking of Step 11 that became routine rather than genuine pursuit. The area of the will that was surrendered in public and quietly reclaimed in private. The examination of Step 12 goes there. Not to condemn. To clarify. Because the commission of Step 12 — the carrying of the message, the leading of others — is only as effective as the life behind it is genuinely examined and genuinely real.

Daily Self-Examination As Discipline

Step 10 built the daily inventory. Step 12 asks for something deeper — the periodic examination of the whole life against the standard of 2 Corinthians 13:5. Not just what went wrong today. Whether the faith itself is real. Whether Jesus Christ is actually in the life — operating, directing, producing — or whether the life is operating under the appearance of faith while the reality is the old self-management in religious clothing. “But let a man examine himself.” — 1 Corinthians 11:28 KJV This discipline — regular, honest, deep self-examination — is what prevents the drift from genuine faith to religious performance. It is what the commissioned life requires before it can produce in others what it is supposed to carry. Examine yourselves. Before you examine anyone else. Prove your own selves. Before you lead anyone through a proof they have not seen you pass first. They overcame him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony. That is the pattern of Step 12. Not by performance. Not by perfection. By what God has done, and the honest account of it.

Nineveh — Jonah And The Prolonged Struggle

God told Jonah to go to Nineveh. Jonah went to Tarshish instead — the opposite direction. And the running produced exactly the storm that the running was meant to avoid. “But Jonah rose up to flee unto Tarshish from the presence of the LORD, and went down to Joppa; and he found a ship going to Tarshish: so he paid the fare thereof, and went down into it, to go with them unto Tarshish from the presence of the LORD.” — Jonah 1:3 KJV Three times in one verse: from the presence of the LORD. The running was not from the assignment. It was from the presence. And every step of the flight — down to Joppa, down into the ship, down into the sea, down into the fish — involved descent. Running from God's assignment is always downward. The self-examination of Step 12 includes this question: is there a Nineveh in your life? An assignment, a calling, a clear direction that God has been giving that you have been running from — perhaps under the cover of needing more time, more stability, more healing? The person who has worked eleven steps honestly and arrived at Step 12 has received significant healing. The question is not whether more is available. The question is whether the running is still happening. Jonah's struggle lasted precisely as long as his resistance. Purpose and freedom are connected. Avoiding God's assignment does not protect you from the difficulty it involves. It only changes the form the difficulty takes. The person who has walked all twelve steps honestly is not the person who arrived at Step 1. That is not an exaggeration — it is the testimony Scripture promises to those who walk the road faithfully. They overcame. Not they tried to overcome. They overcame.
