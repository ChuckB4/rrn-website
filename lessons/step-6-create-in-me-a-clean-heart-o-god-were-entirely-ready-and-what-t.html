Step 6 · Lesson 1 · Free

Create in me a clean heart O God — were entirely ready and what that actually requires

The Covenant Context

David prayed Psalm 51 after Nathan the prophet came to him — after the full weight of what David had done had been named, seen, and stood in. The prayer was not the prayer of a person who had improved sufficiently to ask for the blessing. It was the prayer of a person who had seen clearly what was there and understood that what was there could not be removed by human effort. Create in me a clean heart — not: I will work on cleaning my heart. Create. Do what I cannot do. Give what I cannot manufacture. This is the prayer of Step 6. “Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.” — Psalm 51:10 KJV The Step 5 confession showed what the heart contained. Step 6 is the willingness — the entirely ready posture — for God to create what the confession revealed cannot be produced from within. Not the management of the defects. Not the reduction of the most visible ones. The creation of what was not there before: a clean heart, a right spirit. In Step 5 we saw that what had been kept hidden had power precisely because it was hidden. Step 6 asks a different kind of willingness entirely. The vessel cleared in Step 6 is not cleared so that it remains empty. An empty vessel waiting is the condition described by Jesus when He spoke of the swept house — cleaned but unoccupied, and therefore vulnerable to what returns. The clearing of Step 6 is preparation for what belongs in the vessel. And what belongs in the cleared vessel is not a concept — it is a person, who comes in a name. Readiness to have the defects removed is the inner posture of genuine repentance. It is not enough to name what is wrong and carry it. Repentance that stops at naming has not completed its movement. Step 6 is the turning that makes the request of Step 7 honest.

What Entirely Ready Actually Means

Entirely ready does not mean eager. It means willing — genuinely, completely, without protected reservations. The person who is entirely ready for God to remove their defects has examined each defect and is holding none of them back from the removal. The surprise of Step 6 is that the entirely ready is harder than it looks. The defects the inventory named have been serving a function. The resentment protected from further hurt. The pride prevented the experience of shame. The selfishness ensured the self got what it needed. The dishonesty kept the story manageable. Removing the defect also removes the function the defect was serving — and the flesh does not release functions voluntarily. “Blessed are they which do hunger and thirst after righteousness: for they shall be filled.” — Matthew 5:6 KJV Jesus said this in the Gospels — in the Sermon on the Mount, to the crowd gathered at the beginning of His ministry. The hungering and thirsting describes the intensity of the entirely ready — the person who does not merely prefer to be free of the defects but hungers for it the way a person who has not eaten hungers for food. That intensity is what Step 6 is asking for. Jacob wrestled with God through the night — broken, exhausted, holding on — and received both the wound and the blessing in the same encounter. Readiness does not require strength. It requires surrender.

Why Willpower Cannot Produce It

The defects named in the inventory are not defects the person can simply decide to stop having. The resentment does not resolve because the person decides it should. The selfishness does not disappear because the person identifies it as a problem. The inventory showed the pattern. The pattern runs deeper than decision. “I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me.” — Philippians 4:13 KJV Paul wrote this to born-again believers — describing the strength that operates in the person who has genuinely received what God promises the obedient. The all things includes the removal of what willpower cannot reach. The strengthening is the work of Christ in the person who is entirely ready — not instead of human effort, but as the power that makes possible what human effort cannot produce alone.

The Road Forward

This reveals a pattern that runs through every story of genuine transformation in Scripture: the person brings what they have — the willingness — and God does what only He can do. Human readiness and divine action. Both are required. Neither alone is sufficient. The nature of entirely ready has been established. Lesson 2 addresses the specific dynamics of the flesh and the Spirit in the context of Step 6 — why the willingness requires what it requires and what the Spirit produces when the willingness is genuine. Readiness is itself a form of identity. The person who is entirely ready has made a declaration about who they are becoming — a person whose character is no longer self-governed, but yielded. That posture is what Step 7 walks into. Readiness leads directly to the request. Steps 6 and 7 are two halves of one movement — and what Step 7 asks for is what makes everything prior sustainable.
